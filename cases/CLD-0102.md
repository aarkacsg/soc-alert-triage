# CLD-0102 — Credential-Stealing Malware on an Endpoint

## Alert
Endpoint protection detected credential-stealing malware on a Windows workstation. Multiple detections occurred within a short period, and quarantine failed repeatedly.

## Hypotheses
**Malicious:** The endpoint is actively infected and the malware is persisting or recreating itself.

**Benign:** The alert may represent a stale or erroneous detection that has already been remediated.

## Evidence Reviewed
- Defender AV detection logs
- Four repeated malware detections
- Quarantine failed on each attempt
- The malicious file reappeared under a second filename
- The affected user’s sign-in evidence showed no clear anomalous account activity in the available data

## Verdict
**True Positive**

## Severity
**Sev 2**

The malware was confirmed on a single endpoint and showed persistence-like behaviour. Because the detection involved a credential stealer, there was also potential account-compromise risk even though no suspicious sign-in activity had yet been identified.

## Action
- Isolate the affected workstation
- Escalate for malware eradication or reimage
- Reset the affected user’s credentials
- Revoke active sessions
- Monitor the account for anomalous authentication activity

## Justification
Repeated failed quarantine attempts and recreation of the malicious executable confirmed that the infection was active rather than a stale detection.

## MITRE ATT&CK
**T1204.002 — User Execution: Malicious File**

## Key Learning
A successful malware detection does not automatically mean the threat has been contained. Repeated failed quarantine attempts and file recreation are strong indicators that further containment and escalation are required.

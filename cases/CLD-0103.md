# CLD-0103 — Authorised Vulnerability Scan

## Alert
A network detection identified a large internal TCP port sweep from a known scanning host across many internal systems.

## Hypotheses
**Malicious:** The scanner host may have been compromised or used for unauthorised internal reconnaissance.

**Benign:** The activity may be part of an approved vulnerability-scanning window.

## Evidence Reviewed
- Shift handover and approved scan schedule
- Source host and IP matched the authorised vulnerability scanner
- Activity occurred within the approved scan window
- Traffic consisted of sequential SYN probes across many hosts
- No data transfer or malicious follow-on activity was observed

## Verdict
**False Positive**

## Severity
**Informational**

Although the behaviour resembled internal reconnaissance, the source, timing and traffic pattern matched an approved vulnerability scan.

## Action
- Close the incident
- Reference the approved scanning schedule
- Keep the detection rule active
- Consider suppressing alerts only for the authorised scanner during approved scan windows

## Justification
The source host, IP address, timing and network pattern all matched the organisation’s approved vulnerability-scanning activity, with no evidence of malicious follow-on behaviour.

## MITRE ATT&CK
Not applicable because the alert was determined to be a false positive.

## Key Learning
Known tools and systems should still be investigated when they trigger security alerts. A recognised scanner should not be trusted automatically; its identity, schedule and behaviour must all match the approved activity before closing the alert.
